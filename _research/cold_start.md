summary: "Shows that to overcome the cold start problem, newcomers in underground markets deliberately decide to trade in public instead of privately."
abstract: "Anonymous traders in underground markets need to establish trust, and newcomers cannot rely on reputation to do so. We study one tool available to them: making the terms of a contract publicly visible. Hack Forums introduced a contract system in June 2018 in which contracts are private by default, but the contract maker can make the terms visible to all forum members. Using 205,869 contracts with observed visibility, together with each participant's trading history at the time of each contract, we document three patterns. First, public contracts are used mainly with newcomers rather than by them: visibility falls sharply with the experience of the accepting party, even when the maker is highly experienced. Second, experienced accepters take almost every private offer but decline public offers markedly more often, particularly when the offer comes from a newcomer, while new accepters show no such reluctance. Third, visibility is persistent: pairs and users who have traded publicly before are much more likely to do so again. These patterns hold both while contracts were optional and after the forum made them mandatory in March 2019. The mandate coincided with a sharp fall in public contracting across all experience categories, after which visibility was used almost only when the accepting party lacked a track record. The results are consistent with transparency serving as an accountability device for trading with strangers, one that complements rather than replaces market-level trust mechanisms. They also imply that public contracts are an unrepresentative window onto what is traded in such markets."
